MADRAS, 12th March — Merchants and shipping agents along the Coromandel Coast are reporting a marked improvement in commercial activity this season, with cargo traffic between Madras and Cochin increasing considerably over the past quarter. The Port Commissioner at Cochin has confirmed that spice exports, chiefly pepper and cardamom dispatched from the Malabar hinterlands, have surpassed last year’s figures by nearly eighteen per cent.

Travellers arriving by rail from Trichinopoly and Tanjore speak of a countryside in reasonable spirits, despite the irregular monsoon that troubled farmers in the interior districts last August. The cotton mills of Coimbatore have resumed full operations, and several new looms have been commissioned in anticipation of orders arriving from the mills of Lancashire.

In Bangalore — which the local populace has long called by other names — the civil administration has approved plans for the widening of certain thoroughfares in the cantonment area, a project long overdue according to senior officers stationed there. The cooler climate of Bangalore continues to attract officers and their families seeking respite from the oppressive heat of the plains.

Our correspondent in Calicut reports that the ancient port city, once the great entrepôt of Arab and Chinese traders, is witnessing a modest revival of the timber trade, with teak being floated down from the forests of Malabar and loaded onto vessels bound for Bombay. From Bombay, much of this timber finds its way to the furniture workshops of Europe.

Further north, Poona remains the seat of the seasonal administration, its wide avenues and clubs giving it an air quite unlike the noise and press of Bombay, some hundred and twenty miles distant. The Governor’s residence at Poona is currently hosting a series of meetings between district collectors and revenue officers, the subject being the revision of land assessment rates across the Deccan.

Word from Allahabad suggests that the legal fraternity there is engaged in considerable debate over a proposed amendment to certain provisions of civil procedure — a matter of no small interest to the merchant communities of both Calcutta and Bombay, whose commercial disputes so frequently find their way before the High Courts.

In Calcutta, meanwhile, the jute mills on the Hooghly have returned to working at full capacity following a brief stoppage last month attributed to labour unrest. The Chamber of Commerce in Calcutta has issued a statement urging moderation on all sides and emphasising the importance of uninterrupted production to the broader economy of Bengal.

Readers interested in conditions along the southern tip of the peninsula will note our separate column today concerning affairs in Trivandrum, where the Maharaja’s government has commissioned a new road connecting the capital to the coastal town of Quilon, a project expected to ease considerably the movement of coir and coconut oil to the port.
